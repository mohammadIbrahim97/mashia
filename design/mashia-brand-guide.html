01 · CONCEPT

Why green.

Both Syria and Saudi Arabia carry green at the heart of their flags — not as decoration, but as inheritance. Green threads through Islamic history, fertile river valleys, and the deserts that bloom after rain. For a brand named Mashia — Arabic for desire, what is wanted — green is the natural anchor: rooted, generous, and unmistakably regional.

#007A3D

Syrian Green

The green of the Syrian flag, evoking the Umayyad period and tied through national identity to fertile land and historical continuity.

#006C35

Saudi Green

The deep green of the Saudi flag, symbol of Islam and the kingdom's enduring identity. Slightly cooler and deeper than its Syrian counterpart.

→

Mashia Green sits between the two — a deliberate bridge color (#007339) that reads as native in both Damascus and Riyadh. Not a borrowed green. A shared one.

٣٨ ريال
Accessibility note

Mashia 600 on Paper passes WCAG AA for normal text (contrast ≈ 5.2:1). Avoid Mashia 400 / 500 on white for body copy — use only for large headings or non-text UI. Qasioun Gold on Basalt Black passes AA. Test every accent on a real surface before shipping.

The Mashia palette is inspired by the Syrian National Visual Identity unveiled July 2025 (Qasioun Gold, Ebla Bronze, Palmyra Sand, Orontes Green, Mediterranean Blue, Basalt Black) and by the shared green of the Syrian and Saudi national flags. Heritage accent HEX values in this guide are visual approximations of the named colors — once the official Syrian Identity brand book HEX values become publicly available, replace the values in :root to match. Mashia is an independent project and is not affiliated with either national brand program.
